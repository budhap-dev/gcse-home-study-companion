## What the specifications assess

**Language 8700.** Two papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks, 50% of the GCSE. Paper 1, *Explorations in Creative Reading and Writing*: Section A reads one literature fiction text (a 4-mark short question, two 8-mark questions, one 20-mark evaluation); Section B is one piece of descriptive or narrative writing (24 marks for content and organisation, 16 for technical accuracy). Paper 2, *Writers' Viewpoints and Perspectives*: Section A reads two linked non-fiction texts, one 19th-century and one 20th- or 21st-century (4, 8, 12 and 16 marks); Section B is one piece of writing presenting a viewpoint (24 + 16). Assessment objectives AO1 to AO6 for reading and writing, weighted 10, 17.5, 10, 12.5, 30 and 20 per cent; AO7 to AO9 for the separately endorsed Spoken Language assessment, which is reported but does not count towards the grade.

**Literature 8702.** Closed book, untiered. Paper 1, *Shakespeare and the 19th-century novel*: 1 hour 45 minutes, 64 marks, 40%; Section A one extract-based question on the play, Section B one extract-based question on the novel. Paper 2, *Modern texts and poetry*: 2 hours 15 minutes, 96 marks, 60%; Section A one essay from a choice of two on the modern text, Section B one comparative question on a named printed poem and one other from the cluster, Section C one question on an unseen poem and one comparing it with a second unseen poem. Assessment objectives AO1 to AO4, weighted 37.5 / 42.5 / 15 / 5 across the qualification.

## Authoring notes

Quotations must be verifiable. Shakespeare, Stevenson, and the six public-domain poems (Ozymandias, London, the Prelude extract, My Last Duchess, The Charge of the Light Brigade, Exposure) come from a public-domain edition; Priestley and the nine copyrighted poems only where an AQA paper or mark scheme prints the passage (the Literature Paper 2 poetry question prints one named poem in full each series, and the mark schemes' indicative content quotes the texts). The Literature papers are closed book, so every text topic needs a small bank of exact, checked quotations. Language questions are marked to AQA's levels of response; the app's extended questions carry those level descriptors as criteria, and everything that can be marked automatically (terminology, quotation recall, true-or-false statements, the order of a plot, the form a task asks for) is a multiple-choice, short-text or ordering question.
